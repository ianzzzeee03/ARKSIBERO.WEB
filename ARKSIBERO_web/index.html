01THE GAME

A STORY SHAPED BY CHOICE

The truth is
never one-sided.

In Manila, 1899, the Philippine-American War is being reported from every side. As a young Filipino journalist, you’ll gather testimony, follow paper trails, and ask questions people would rather leave alone.

There’s no simple choice between right and wrong. You decide whose account to trust, what the evidence can support, and what you’re willing to risk when the presses start rolling.

FIELD NOTE NO. 04

AT YOUR DESK

Three kinds of source.
None tell the whole story.

A / THE WITNESS

What someone remembers

Ask where they were, what they saw themselves, and what they heard from someone else. A careful witness can still be mistaken.

B / THE PAPER TRAIL

What the record says

Read telegrams, lists, and printed notices closely. Check who made them, when they moved, and what has been crossed out.

C / THE OFFICIAL ACCOUNT

What authority reports

Official statements can offer useful detail—and reflect the interests of the office that issued them. Look for independent confirmation.

!

Choices have a paper trail. A source you protect may speak again. A detail you print may put someone at risk. The game remembers who you believed and what you left out.

5 NOTES / 5 HIDDEN DETAILS
THE PERIOD, NOT THE PLOT1899

News traveled fast.
Certainty traveled faster.

There were no feeds or instant updates. Reports moved by telegraph and cable, then by newspaper reprints, letters, and word of mouth. Every handoff took time—and gave someone another chance to shorten, translate, or frame the story. A fabricated claim or distorted detail could be repeated widely before a correction caught up.

01 / THE FIRST VERSIONDistance from the event

Readers often depended on a correspondent or official dispatch rather than a witness they could question themselves.

02 / THE REPRINTAuthority by repetition

When several papers repeated the same account, it could look independently confirmed even when they shared one source.

03 / THE EDITA claim loses its edges

A cautious phrase could disappear in a headline, a translation, or a rushed summary. What began as a possibility could arrive as fact.

This is historical context for the game’s themes, not a claim that every 1899 report was false. The characters, records, and clues below are fictional.
